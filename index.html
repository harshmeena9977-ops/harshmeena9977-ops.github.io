01 / ANALYST THESIS

A dashboard is not the answer.

The decision it enables is.

I don't want my portfolio to tell you that I know SQL, Python or Power BI. I want the work to make that obvious.

Each project starts with a business question, moves through messy data and ends with a finding, recommendation or action.

"The interesting part isn't finding the number. It's understanding why the number moved."

02 / SELECTED INVESTIGATIONS

Four datasets.
Four business questions.

Investigation / Retention

Month-to-month customers were leaving at roughly 43% — making contract structure the clearest retention signal.

Started with 7,043 customer records, cleaned the data in Python and queried customer segments using SQL. A Random Forest model was then used to identify customers with elevated churn risk.

The final Power BI dashboard turns those findings into a practical retention target list rather than another collection of charts.

26.5% overall churn
43% month-to-month churn
85.3% model accuracy
82.1% precision
Investigation / Workforce

Overtime employees showed a 2.93× higher attrition signal than the baseline.

Analysed 1,470 employee records using Power Query, DAX and Power BI. The work focused on discovering which employee segments were contributing most to workforce loss.

Instead of presenting attrition as one KPI, the dashboard breaks it into department, role, age, income and overtime patterns.

Inspect repository ↗
1,470 employees analysed
16.12% attrition rate
2.93× overtime risk signal
35.77% under-25 attrition
Investigation / Profitability

Profitability was not evenly distributed: a small number of regional/category segments created a disproportionate drag.

Cleaned and analysed 10,000+ transactions across regions and product categories. SQL aggregation and cross-tab analysis were used to isolate the low-profit segments.

The reporting workflow was then converted into an automated executive dashboard, reducing repetitive month-end reporting work.

Inspect repository ↗
10K+ transactions
4 regions
20% profitability drag
65% reporting effort reduced
Investigation / Consumer Behaviour

A small group of elite users generated a surprisingly large share of the platform's review activity.

Processed 150K+ restaurant records and 2M+ reviews from the Yelp Open Dataset. SQL and Python were used to join, clean and segment restaurant and reviewer behaviour.

The analysis surfaced an engagement gap between high- and low-rated restaurants and identified elite reviewers as a potentially high-leverage engagement segment.
